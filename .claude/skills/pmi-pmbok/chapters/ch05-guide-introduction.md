# Chapter 5: Introduction (A Guide to the PMBOK)

## Core Idea
The 8th edition PMBOK Guide is a structural merger: it combines the 6th edition's detailed, process-oriented ITTO (Inputs, Tools & Techniques, Outputs) approach with the 7th edition's Performance Domains concept, integrating ITTOs and process descriptions *directly into* the performance-domain structure — giving both per-process detail and a systems view of how domains interconnect.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Principles → Performance Domains relationship (Figure 1-1)**: the six Standard principles (grouped into Proactive/Ownership/Value-Driven mindset dimensions) form the foundation that guides behavior *within* the seven performance domains (Governance, Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources, Risk). Principles = mindset/behavior; Performance Domains = where that mindset is demonstrated in practice.
  - When to use: when a performance domain's guidance seems to conflict with "doing the right thing," trace back to which principle should be governing the decision — the domains are mechanics, the principles are intent.
- **Guide structure, four core sections**: (1) Project Management Performance Domains — seven domains forming an integrated system; (2) Tailoring — what/how to tailor; (3) Inputs and Outputs — sample (not comprehensive, not mandatory) assets/artifacts used across domains; (4) Tools and Techniques — sample (not comprehensive, not mandatory) tools/techniques used across domains.
  - Important: Sections 3 and 4 are explicitly **not exhaustive and not required** — treat ITTO lists as a reference menu, not a checklist to fully complete on every project.

## Key Concepts
- **Tailoring**: the deliberate adaptation of project management approach, governance, and processes to fit a given environment (industry, culture, regulations, etc.) and the purpose of the work — driven by the guiding principles plus organizational values/culture.
- **Performance domain**: a broad area of focus in which principle-driven mindset and behavior are demonstrated through processes.

## Mental Models
- Read any PMBOK Guide process or tool as "a commonly used good practice that fits most projects most of the time" — not as a mandatory universal procedure. The guide explicitly disclaims completeness.
- When in doubt whether something belongs in "principles" or "performance domains," ask: is this a *why/how-to-think* (principle) or a *where-to-apply-it* (domain)?

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating the ITTO lists in Sections 3–4 as mandatory or exhaustive**: the guide explicitly states these are illustrative, commonly used selections — not a compliance checklist.
- **Applying performance-domain mechanics without checking principle alignment**: Figure 1-1 exists specifically because domains are meant to operationalize principles, not substitute for them.

## Key Takeaways
1. The 8th edition deliberately fuses 6th-edition process/ITTO detail with 7th-edition performance-domain systems thinking — expect both levels of guidance throughout the Guide.
2. Treat all ITTO and tools/techniques content as a reference menu of common practices, not a required checklist.
3. Tailoring is principle-driven, not just context-driven — any tailoring decision should still trace back to the six Standard principles.
