# Chapter 11: Resources Performance Domain

## Core Idea
Resources — human (the team) and physical/virtual (equipment, materials, facilities, software, licenses, etc.) — need distinct skill sets to manage: leading people is fundamentally different from acquiring/controlling things, and this domain's job is ensuring the right resources are available at the right time and place, then releasing them when no longer needed.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Management vs. Leadership activities** (Lead the Team): Management = means of meeting objectives (processes, planning, coordinating, measuring, monitoring); Leadership = people-focused (influencing, motivating, listening, enabling). A PM needs both and should adjust style to team members' willingness/ability.
- **High-Performing Team factors** (2.6.2.4.2): open communication, shared understanding, shared ownership, trust, collaboration, adaptability, resilience, empowerment/delegation/autonomy, recognition — a checklist for diagnosing team health.
- **Dialogue vs. Debate**: dialogue = working together toward a resolution all parties can embrace; debate = win-lose, more interested in winning than finding the best solution. Use dialogue as the default mode for resolving diverging opinions.
- **The Project Manager's culture-building behaviors**: transparency (including about one's own biases), integrity, respect, positive discourse (dialogue not debate), support, courage, celebrating success — the specific, modelable behaviors a PM uses to build a safe, high-trust team environment.

## Key Concepts
- **Resource manager**: has management authority over resources; the project team may not control resource selection directly (collective bargaining, subcontractors, matrix environments). Internal resources come from functional/resource managers; external resources come via partnerships/joint ventures/procurement.
- **Human resources**: individuals with roles/responsibilities working toward a shared goal — may be top-down led (centralized) or self-organizing (distributed/decentralized leadership).
- **Physical/material/virtual resources**: anything that isn't a person — equipment, materials, supplies, facilities, infrastructure, software, testing environments, licenses, services.
- **Quiet quitting**: employees doing the minimum to keep their job without going the extra mile — a named well-being/engagement risk signal (reluctance to speak up, not volunteering, higher absenteeism).
- **Resource-based view, green human resource management, SWOT analysis**: named tools/techniques for Plan Resource Management.

## Mental Models
- Team maturity changes oversight needs: mature/experienced teams need less direct leadership; new-to-org/new-to-specialty members need more.
- Cross-organizational teams (contract/partnership-formed) need deliberate "one team" investment up front — roles may be more formalized/less flexible due to contract terms, so don't assume organic team cohesion will just happen.
- Treat resource competition across concurrent projects as a real constraint, not a scheduling footnote — it directly impacts cost, schedule, risk, scope, and quality simultaneously.
- Sustainability applies across the entire resource value chain — hiring, sourcing, transportation, and disposal — not just the project's direct deliverable.

## Anti-patterns
- **Failing to secure critical equipment/infrastructure on time** → delays in final-product manufacture.
- **Ordering low-quality materials to save cost** → damages product quality, drives up recalls/rework.
- **Over-provisioning inventory** → high operational cost, reduced profit; **under-provisioning** → can't meet customer demand, also reduces profit. Both directions of the inventory-level mistake are named risks.
- **Ignoring virtual-team collaboration gaps**: assuming remote connection effort automatically replicates in-person collaboration quality — it doesn't without deliberate technology and relationship-building investment.
- **Treating "quiet quitting" as a performance problem rather than an engagement signal**: it's specifically named as a well-being/culture issue to watch for, not just an individual productivity issue.

## Worked Example

**Newly formed virtual team (2.6.3.1, Example 2)**: Suggested fixes for collaboration issues — set up shared collaboration sites, maintain a project team information hub, use audio/video/virtual conferencing for meetings, use messaging/texting for ongoing contact, deliberately schedule time to get to know remote teammates, hold at least one face-to-face meeting to establish relationships, and create a team charter defining ways of working. This is a concrete checklist, not just "communicate more."

## Key Takeaways
1. Separate management activities (process/means) from leadership activities (people/motivation) — both are needed, and the right mix shifts with team maturity.
2. Use the high-performing-team checklist (open communication, shared understanding/ownership, trust, collaboration, adaptability, resilience, empowerment, recognition) as a diagnostic, not just an aspiration.
3. Default to dialogue over debate when resolving team disagreements — the goal is a resolution everyone can embrace, not a winner.
4. Watch both ends of resource-level mismanagement — too much inventory/resource hoarding and too little are both named failure modes.
5. Virtual teams need deliberate technology AND relationship investment (including at least one face-to-face meeting) — distance collaboration doesn't self-organize into trust.
6. Treat quiet quitting and burnout signals as engagement-domain data, not just individual performance issues.
