# Chapter 32: GPT-4

## Core Idea
GPT-4 advances the GPT lineage along three axes simultaneously — multimodality (image + text input, text output), benchmark performance (human-level on many professional/academic exams, e.g., top-10% bar exam score vs. GPT-3.5's bottom-10%), and safety alignment (82% less likely to respond to disallowed content, 40% more factual than GPT-3.5, after 6 months of adversarial testing) — while still falling short of humans in many real-world scenarios.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **GPT-4's Three-Axis Advancement Model**: Multimodality (visual + textual reasoning, including Visual Question Answering) / Benchmark Performance (academic + professional exams, SOTA ML model comparisons) / Safety Alignment (6-month adversarial testing program, expert-reviewed, classifier-iterated monitoring).
  - When to use: evaluating any claimed "next-generation" LLM release against a consistent, three-dimensional rubric rather than a single headline metric.
  - How: check (1) does it handle new input modalities, (2) how does it score on standardized external benchmarks versus the predecessor and other SOTA models, (3) what specific, quantified safety improvements were made and how were they tested.

## Key Concepts
- **Multimodal input**: GPT-4 accepts both image and text inputs while emitting text-only outputs — a structural change from the text-only prior generation.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA)**: GPT-4's ability to perform visual reasoning/logic extraction from a picture with accuracy comparable to its textual-data performance.
- **Bar-exam benchmark**: GPT-4 scored in the top 10% of test-takers on a simulated bar exam, versus GPT-3.5's bottom 10% — the chapter's signature before/after capability illustration.
- **Adversarial testing program**: a 6-month iterative alignment process incorporating lessons from real-world ChatGPT use and over 50 external domain experts (including AI safety specialists).
- **Quantified safety gains**: 82% reduction in responding to disallowed-content requests; 40% improvement in factual response rate, both relative to GPT-3.5.

## Mental Models
- Use the **"capability gain must be paired with a safety gain" expectation** as a vetting standard for any major model release — the chapter explicitly reports both dimensions together (performance AND alignment), modeling how a credible model announcement should be evaluated.
- Treat **"human-level on exams, still below humans in real-world scenarios" as a necessary caveat** whenever interpreting any benchmark-score headline — strong standardized-test performance does not automatically transfer to robust real-world task performance.

## Key Takeaways
1. GPT-4's multimodality (image+text in, text out) is a structural capability expansion, not just a scale increase over GPT-3.5.
2. The bar-exam score jump (bottom 10% → top 10%) is the chapter's most concrete, memorable illustration of generational capability improvement.
3. Safety work is presented as a parallel, quantified engineering effort (6 months, 50+ experts, 82%/40% improvement figures) — not an afterthought bolted onto a capability-only release.
4. As of the chapter's writing (shortly after the March 14 launch), many technical details remained undisclosed and the model's full domain-by-domain utilization was still unexplored.
