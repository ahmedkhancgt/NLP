## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The Project Management Mindset (3 dimensions, 6 principles)** — Proactive (Adopt a Holistic View + Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables), Ownership (Be an Accountable Leader + Build an Empowered Culture), Value-Driven (Focus on Value + Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas). Use this as a first diagnostic lens on any struggling project: is the gap proactive/planning, ownership/accountability, or value/sustainability?

**Principles → Performance Domains**: principles guide mindset/behavior; performance domains (Governance, Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources, Risk) are where that mindset is demonstrated mechanically. All six principles touch all seven domains, with uneven strength (see ch03's cross-map).

**Value delivery system**: Portfolio Management + Strategic Planning sit at the center, linking Organizational Strategy to Management of Programs/Projects and Operations/Products, inside an Internal Environment inside an External Environment — closed-loop feedback (not just top-down delivery) is what makes it work.

**Development Approach Spectrum**: Predictive ↔ Hybrid ↔ Adaptive. Select using the deliverables/project/organization factor checklist (ch04) — never default by habit. The **inverted triangle**: in adaptive work, explicitly fix either (budget+schedule) or (scope) and let the other flex.

**Five Project Management Focus Areas** (not phases): Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Controlling, Closing — recur and overlap throughout every phase/iteration, regardless of development approach.

**Governance's three mandatory components**: target metrics tied to strategy + signaling/leading-indicator mechanisms + feedback mechanisms. Missing any one = incomplete governance. Structured governance (sponsor/PMO/board/PM) fits predictive/large projects; self-governance (distributed accountability) fits adaptive, but needs clear objectives + indicators + feedback to avoid fragmented decisions.

**Quality Assurance ≠ Quality Control**: QA = trusting the *process* (audits, compliance); QC = verifying the *deliverable* (testing, specs). Keep them as separate tracks in any quality plan.

**Scope = where value lives**: scope is "the most important component of any project's baseline." Quality (functional + nonfunctional requirements) is a *feature of scope*, not separate from it. Use a Value Breakdown Structure (VBS) alongside the WBS to assign explicit value to deliverables before decomposing — this drives honest prioritization instead of task-size-driven prioritization.

**Measurement Pitfalls** (watch for all six before trusting any dashboard): Hawthorne effect, vanity metrics, demoralization, misusing metrics, confirmation bias, correlation-vs-causation. Every metric should pass the SMART test first.

**Two-dimension success assessment**: judge every project on outcome success (value realized) AND process success (cost/schedule/quality adherence) *separately* — they can diverge sharply (Sydney Opera House = process failure/outcome success; Montreal overpass = process success/outcome failure).
