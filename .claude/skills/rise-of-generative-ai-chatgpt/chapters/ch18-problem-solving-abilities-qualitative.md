# Chapter 18: Problem solving abilities (Qualitative)

## Core Idea
Qualitative problem-solving — understanding underlying principles/root causes rather than symptoms, via multi-perspective analysis — is an area where ChatGPT can "grasp concepts and build on top of them" even though it lacks genuine human-level qualitative judgment, since its opinions are ultimately just patterns collected from training data.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Qualitative problem-solving framework**: break a problem into fundamental components → analyze from multiple perspectives → identify root causes (not just symptoms) → synthesize a holistic understanding, often using observational/interview/case-study-style data.
  - When to use: research, management, or corporate problems where the underlying causes are not immediately apparent.
  - How: prompt ChatGPT to (1) decompose the problem, (2) offer multiple interpretive angles, (3) propose a research outline or structured approach rather than a single "answer."

## Key Concepts
- **Root-cause vs. symptom-treatment**: the chapter's central qualitative-advantage framing — ChatGPT-assisted analysis aims at underlying causes, not surface symptoms.
- **Multi-perspective examination**: generating several different interpretations of the same problem or philosophical question (demonstrated with Aristotle's philosophy) to widen the research/analysis angle.
- **Foundational output vs. human-level judgment**: the chapter's explicit caveat that ChatGPT's qualitative "opinion" is pattern-derived, not genuine reasoning.

## Mental Models
- Use the **"foundational output, not final judgment" framing** — treat ChatGPT's qualitative analysis as a fast first pass that surfaces angles a researcher/manager can then evaluate and refine, not as the conclusion itself.
- Treat **generating multiple interpretations as a deliberate technique**, not a limitation — asking for several different takes on the same philosophical/conceptual question is explicitly used here as a way to widen a research project's scope.

## Worked Example
The chapter presents three mini-cases: (1) solving a logical-reasoning flow rapidly, (2) generating a research outline for a sentiment-analysis review project, (3) producing four distinct interpretations of Aristotle's philosophy — together demonstrating logic-solving, research-scaffolding, and multi-perspective conceptual analysis as the three facets of "qualitative" assistance.

## Key Takeaways
1. ChatGPT's qualitative strength lies in generating multiple research-ready framings/outlines quickly, not in delivering authoritative final interpretations.
2. Root-cause-oriented prompting (ask "why" iteratively) extracts more value than single-shot factual questions for qualitative work.
3. The "foundational output" caveat is essential: qualitative outputs require human evaluation before being treated as conclusions, mirroring the human-oversight principle from Ch 6.
4. Multi-perspective generation (several interpretations of one concept) is a specific, reusable technique for research brainstorming.
