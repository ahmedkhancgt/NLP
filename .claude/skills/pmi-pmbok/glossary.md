# Glossary — PMBOK Guide, 8th Edition (as captured)

**Artifact** — A document or other item created during a portfolio, program, or project to help manage it and provide information to the team, stakeholders, and management. (Ch 1)

**Benefit** — A gain or asset realized by the organization and other stakeholders as the result of outcomes delivered. (Ch 1)

**Business case** — A documented economic feasibility study establishing the validity of benefits to be delivered by a portfolio component, program, or project; outlines costs, benefits, value-creation approach, and success criteria. (Ch 7)

**Change Control Board (CCB)** — A formally established group responsible for reviewing, evaluating, approving, deferring, or rejecting project changes, and documenting/communicating those decisions. Used in predictive approaches. (Ch 6)

**Definition of Done (DoD)** — A checklist of criteria required for a deliverable (or user story/feature/increment) to be considered ready for customer use/release. (Ch 7)

**Delivery cadence** — The timing pattern of value delivery: single, multiple, periodic, or continuous. (Ch 4)

**Development approach** — The method (predictive, adaptive, or hybrid) used to create and evolve a product, service, or result during the project life cycle — distinct from "development phase." (Ch 4)

**Disbenefit** — A negative outcome or consequence of a project, as opposed to a benefit. (Ch 2)

**Enterprise Environmental Factors (EEFs)** — Conditions not directly influenced by the project team, internal or external to the organization, that impact, constrain, or direct the project. (Ch 2)

**Escalation** — A governance component routing decisions/blockers to higher organizational authority; common in hierarchical, predictive environments. (Ch 6)

**Explicit knowledge** — Formal, systematic, codifiable knowledge (manuals, procedures, documented processes) that's easily shared via information tools. (Ch 6)

**Governance (project)** — The adaptable framework, functions, and processes that guide project management decisions and activities to optimize value delivery; holistic and integrative across all performance domains. (Ch 1, Ch 6)

**Governance (organizational)** — Direction and control through policies, processes, procedures, and decisions to meet strategic/operational goals, typically overseen by an executive committee or organizational leaders. (Ch 1)

**Hybrid approach** — A development approach combining predictive and adaptive elements, at Levels 1 (predictive-dominant), 2 (balanced), or 3 (adaptive-dominant) per PMI Disciplined Agile. (Ch 4)

**Inverted triangle concept** — The idea that under adaptive approaches, one constraint set (budget+schedule, or scope) must be fixed while the other flexes to absorb unexpected change. (Ch 4)

**Investment control** — A governance component applying formal stewardship of project funding, common in public companies, government agencies, and lender-financed projects. (Ch 6)

**Lagging indicator** — A measure of project deliverables/events providing information after the fact (e.g., schedule/cost variance, deliverables completed); easier to measure than leading indicators but reactive. (Ch 6)

**Leading indicator** — A measure that reveals upcoming changes or trends before they cross a tolerance threshold, allowing preventive action. (Ch 6)

**Organizational Process Assets (OPAs)** — Internal-to-the-organization policies/processes/procedures and knowledge repositories that influence project management. (Ch 2)

**Outcome** — The end result or consequence of a process or project; can be positive (benefit) or negative (disbenefit). (Ch 1)

**Output** — A product, result, or service generated by a process; may be an input to a successor process. (Ch 1)

**Performance domain** — A group of related, interdependent, interactive processes critical for effective delivery of project value; seven exist (Governance, Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources, Risk), presented without weighting or order. (Ch 5, Ch 6)

**Phase (project)** — A collection of logically related project activities culminating in one or more deliverables/outcomes; may be sequential, overlapping, or transitional. (Ch 4)

**Phase gate** (stage gate / gate review / iteration review / decision point review) — A review at a phase boundary producing a decision: continue, continue with modification, end, remain, repeat, or park. (Ch 4)

**Portfolio** — A collection of programs, projects, and operations managed as a group to maximize value delivery and achieve strategic objectives. (Ch 1)

**Principle (project management)** — A fundamental norm, truth, or value providing guidance for mindset and behavior; this standard defines six, grouped into three mindset dimensions. (Ch 3, Ch 5)

**Procurement management** — The discipline of selecting, contracting, and supervising vendors to perform outsourced work; complementary to project management. (Ch 6)

**Product backlog** — A dynamic, prioritized list of work items/features; the adaptive-environment analog of a WBS. (Ch 7)

**Product scope** — Description of the features, functions, and characteristics of the product/service/result the project will deliver. (Ch 7)

**Program** — A group of related projects and program activities managed in a coordinated manner to obtain benefits unavailable from managing them individually — "not merely large projects." (Ch 1)

**Progressive elaboration** — Ongoing, iterative refinement of the project management plan as more information becomes available. (Ch 4)

**Project** — A temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value. (Ch 1)

**Project charter** — The document that officially authorizes a project's start, linking it to the business case and strategic goals and granting the project manager authority. (Ch 6)

**Project governance model** — Structured (sponsor/PMO/governance board/PM-led) or self-governing (distributed team accountability); choice driven by development approach and complexity. (Ch 6)

**Quality** — The degree to which a deliverable's/process's inherent characteristics meet or exceed target objectives; spans performance, conformity, reliability, resilience, satisfaction, uniformity, efficiency, sustainability, and compliance. (Ch 3)

**Quality assurance** — Using project processes effectively so stakeholders can trust the eventual result; related to regulations, compliance, audits. Distinct from quality control. (Ch 6)

**Quality control** — Creating deliverables that meet defined specifications/thresholds; related to product design, testing, defects. Distinct from quality assurance. (Ch 6)

**Requirement** — A condition or capability necessary in a product/service/result to satisfy a business need. (Ch 7)

**Scope baseline** — In predictive environments, the approved formal scope documents (part of the Performance Measurement Baseline); in adaptive environments, defined per-iteration from prioritized requirements. (Ch 7)

**Scope (project)** — The work performed to deliver a product/service/result with specified features/functions; the most important component of a project's baseline since it encapsulates expected value. (Ch 7)

**SMART criteria** — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound — the bar for setting clear, effective goals/metrics. (Ch 6)

**Sourcing strategy** — The governance decision of whether to insource or outsource project work, considering schedule/scope/finance/resource/risk impacts. (Ch 6)

**Tacit knowledge** — Personal, experience-based knowledge that is difficult to articulate, formalize, or transfer. (Ch 6)

**Tailoring** — The deliberate adaptation of project management approach, governance, and processes to fit a given environment and purpose of work, driven by principles plus organizational values/culture. (Ch 5)

**Value** — The excess of financial and nonfinancial benefits over investment gained from achieving portfolio/program/project goals; perceived differently by different stakeholders. (Ch 1)

**Value Breakdown Structure (VBS)** — A hierarchical structure connecting project scope and intended value to product scope, assigning value estimates to deliverables to drive prioritization. (Ch 7)

**Value delivery system** — The collection of strategic business activities (portfolios, programs, projects, products, operations) that build, sustain, or advance an organization. (Ch 1, Ch 2)

**Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** — A hierarchical decomposition of total project scope into manageable work packages; forms part of the scope baseline in predictive/hybrid approaches. (Ch 7)

**WBS dictionary** — A supporting document elaborating each WBS component's scope description, milestones, responsible parties, resource needs, and acceptance criteria. (Ch 7)
