# Glossary — PMBOK Guide, 8th Edition (as captured)

**Actual Cost (AC)** — The realized cost incurred for work performed on an activity during a specific time period; no upper limit. (Ch 14)

**Agile contracting** — Applying agile principles (flexibility, iterative delivery) to a fundamental contract type, e.g., a T&M contract with iteration-based deliverables. (Ch 15)

**Ambiguity** — A state of being unclear or not knowing what to expect, arising from too many options or lack of clarity; doesn't automatically escalate into a tracked risk. (Ch 12)

**Artifact** — A document or other item created during a portfolio, program, or project to help manage it and provide information to the team, stakeholders, and management. (Ch 1)

**Augmentation (AI)** — The AI-adoption tier for strategic/complex tasks (e.g., portfolio trade-offs) where AI is used as an iterative brainstorming partner, not a one-shot answer source. (Ch 15)

**Automation (AI)** — The AI-adoption tier for low-complexity, low-oversight tasks (report generation, meeting summaries) suited to reusable prompts. (Ch 15)

**Assistance (AI)** — The AI-adoption tier where AI output complements human analysis but is never considered complete without human refinement. (Ch 15)

**Benefit** — A gain or asset realized by the organization and other stakeholders as the result of outcomes delivered. (Ch 1)

**Budget at Completion (BAC)** — The sum of all budgets established for the work to be performed; the total planned value (PV) for the project. (Ch 14)

**Buffer Protection Index (BPI)** — In Critical Chain Project Management, the ratio of remaining project buffer to remaining critical-chain length; protects the due date directly (unlike SPI). (Ch 14)

**Business case** — A documented economic feasibility study establishing the validity of benefits to be delivered by a portfolio component, program, or project; outlines costs, benefits, value-creation approach, and success criteria. (Ch 7)

**CapEx (Capital Expenditure)** — Funds invested to acquire, upgrade, or maintain physical assets (property, equipment, technology). Contrast OpEx. (Ch 9)

**Change Control Board (CCB)** — A formally established group responsible for reviewing, evaluating, approving, deferring, or rejecting project changes, and documenting/communicating those decisions. Used in predictive approaches. (Ch 6)

**Contingency reserve** — Time/money allocated within the cost or schedule baseline for known risks with active response strategies (known-unknowns). Contrast management reserve. (Ch 9, Ch 12)

**Contract types** — Fixed-price (cost risk on seller), cost-reimbursable (cost risk on buyer), Time & Materials/T&M (hybrid), target-cost (shared gain/loss). (Ch 15)

**Cost Performance Index (CPI)** — EV / AC; a measure of cost efficiency — greater than 1.0 means under planned cost. (Ch 14)

**Cost Variance (CV)** — EV − AC; positive = under planned cost, negative = over planned cost. (Ch 14)

**Crashing** — A schedule compression technique that shortens critical-path activity durations by adding resources, at added cost. Contrast fast tracking. (Ch 14)

**Critical Chain Project Management (CCPM)** — Uses critical-path logic but replaces task-level buffers with one project buffer, tracked via the Buffer Protection Index. (Ch 14)

**Critical Path Method (CPM)** — Forward-pass/backward-pass technique identifying the sequence of zero-float activities (the critical path) that determines minimum project duration. (Ch 14)

**Definition of Done (DoD)** — A checklist of criteria required for a deliverable (or user story/feature/increment) to be considered ready for customer use/release. (Ch 7)

**Delivery cadence** — The timing pattern of value delivery: single, multiple, periodic, or continuous. (Ch 4)

**Development approach** — The method (predictive, adaptive, or hybrid) used to create and evolve a product, service, or result during the project life cycle — distinct from "development phase." (Ch 4)

**Dialogue vs. Debate** — Dialogue = working together toward a resolution all parties embrace; debate = win-lose, more about winning than resolving. (Ch 11)

**Disbenefit** — A negative outcome or consequence of a project, as opposed to a benefit. (Ch 2)

**Earned Value (EV)** — The budget value of work actually completed, measured against the Performance Measurement Baseline; cannot exceed authorized PV. (Ch 14)

**Earned Value Management (EVM)** — Integrates scope, cost, and schedule baselines into one Performance Measurement Baseline, tracked via PV/EV/AC and their derived formulas (CV, SV, CPI, SPI, EAC, ETC, VAC, TCPI). (Ch 14)

**Emotional intelligence** — Ability to identify, assess, and manage personal and others' emotions; four quadrants: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, social skills. (Ch 11, Ch 14)

**Enterprise Environmental Factors (EEFs)** — Conditions not directly influenced by the project team, internal or external to the organization, that impact, constrain, or direct the project. (Ch 2)

**Escalation (governance)** — A governance component routing decisions/blockers to higher organizational authority; common in hierarchical, predictive environments. (Ch 6)

**Estimate at Completion (EAC)** — The expected total cost of completing all work; several formulas depending on whether past performance predicts future performance. (Ch 14)

**Estimate to Complete (ETC)** — The expected cost to finish all remaining project work; EAC − AC, or re-estimated bottom-up. (Ch 14)

**Explicit knowledge** — Formal, systematic, codifiable knowledge (manuals, procedures, documented processes) that's easily shared via information tools. (Ch 6)

**Fast tracking** — A schedule compression technique running normally-sequential activities in parallel, at added risk/rework rather than added cost. Contrast crashing. (Ch 14)

**Governance (project)** — The adaptable framework, functions, and processes that guide project management decisions and activities to optimize value delivery; holistic and integrative across all performance domains. (Ch 1, Ch 6)

**Governance (organizational)** — Direction and control through policies, processes, procedures, and decisions to meet strategic/operational goals, typically overseen by an executive committee or organizational leaders. (Ch 1)

**High-performing team factors** — Open communication, shared understanding, shared ownership, trust, collaboration, adaptability, resilience, empowerment, recognition. (Ch 11)

**Hybrid approach** — A development approach combining predictive and adaptive elements, at Levels 1 (predictive-dominant), 2 (balanced), or 3 (adaptive-dominant) per PMI Disciplined Agile. (Ch 4)

**Inverted triangle concept** — The idea that under adaptive approaches, one constraint set (budget+schedule, or scope) must be fixed while the other flexes to absorb unexpected change. (Ch 4)

**Investment control** — A governance component applying formal stewardship of project funding, common in public companies, government agencies, and lender-financed projects. (Ch 6)

**Issue** — A condition that has already occurred and may require immediate action — distinct from a risk (hasn't happened yet). (Ch 12)

**Lagging indicator** — A measure of project deliverables/events providing information after the fact (e.g., schedule/cost variance, deliverables completed); easier to measure than leading indicators but reactive. (Ch 6)

**Leading indicator** — A measure that reveals upcoming changes or trends before they cross a tolerance threshold, allowing preventive action. (Ch 6)

**Make-or-buy analysis** — Decision process (using payback period, ROI, IRR, NPV, cost-benefit) determining whether to insource or outsource work. (Ch 15)

**Management reserve** — Time/money held outside the cost/schedule baseline for unforeseen work (unknown-unknowns), released at senior leadership's discretion. Contrast contingency reserve. (Ch 9, Ch 12)

**Organizational Process Assets (OPAs)** — Internal-to-the-organization policies/processes/procedures and knowledge repositories that influence project management. (Ch 2)

**OpEx (Operational Expenditure)** — Funds for ongoing day-to-day business operations (advertising, admin, wages, rent, utilities). Contrast CapEx. (Ch 9)

**Outcome** — The end result or consequence of a process or project; can be positive (benefit) or negative (disbenefit). (Ch 1)

**Output** — A product, result, or service generated by a process; may be an input to a successor process. (Ch 1)

**Overall risk** — The effect of uncertainty on the portfolio/program/project as a whole, as distinct from individual risks; may lead to project cancellation if too high. (Ch 12)

**Performance domain** — A group of related, interdependent, interactive processes critical for effective delivery of project value; seven exist (Governance, Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources, Risk), presented without weighting or order. (Ch 5, Ch 6)

**Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB)** — The integrated scope, schedule, and cost baselines used for comparison in EVM. (Ch 14)

**Phase (project)** — A collection of logically related project activities culminating in one or more deliverables/outcomes; may be sequential, overlapping, or transitional. (Ch 4)

**Phase gate** (stage gate / gate review / iteration review / decision point review) — A review at a phase boundary producing a decision: continue, continue with modification, end, remain, repeat, or park. (Ch 4)

**Planned Value (PV)** — The authorized budget assigned to scheduled work at a point in time; its project total is BAC. (Ch 14)

**PMO (Project Management Office)** — An organizational entity whose value is judged on both actual delivery (cost/risk/speed) and perceived value to its "customers" (including internal PMs/teams); modern PMOs are typically hybrids of directive/supportive/agile models rather than one pure type. (Ch 15)

**Portfolio** — A collection of programs, projects, and operations managed as a group to maximize value delivery and achieve strategic objectives. (Ch 1)

**Principle (project management)** — A fundamental norm, truth, or value providing guidance for mindset and behavior; this standard defines six, grouped into three mindset dimensions. (Ch 3, Ch 5)

**Procurement management** — The discipline of selecting, contracting, and supervising vendors to perform outsourced work; complementary to project management. (Ch 6, Ch 15)

**Product backlog** — A dynamic, prioritized list of work items/features; the adaptive-environment analog of a WBS. (Ch 7)

**Product scope** — Description of the features, functions, and characteristics of the product/service/result the project will deliver. (Ch 7)

**Program** — A group of related projects and program activities managed in a coordinated manner to obtain benefits unavailable from managing them individually — "not merely large projects." (Ch 1)

**Progressive elaboration** — Ongoing, iterative refinement of the project management plan as more information becomes available. (Ch 4)

**Project** — A temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value. (Ch 1)

**Project charter** — The document that officially authorizes a project's start, linking it to the business case and strategic goals and granting the project manager authority; higher-level than the project scope statement. (Ch 6, Ch 14)

**Project governance model** — Structured (sponsor/PMO/governance board/PM-led) or self-governing (distributed team accountability); choice driven by development approach and complexity. (Ch 6)

**Project resilience** — The ability to absorb impacts and recover quickly from setbacks, black swan events, or unknown-unknown risks. (Ch 12)

**Quality** — The degree to which a deliverable's/process's inherent characteristics meet or exceed target objectives; spans performance, conformity, reliability, resilience, satisfaction, uniformity, efficiency, sustainability, and compliance. (Ch 3)

**Quality assurance** — Using project processes effectively so stakeholders can trust the eventual result; related to regulations, compliance, audits. Distinct from quality control. (Ch 6)

**Quality control** — Creating deliverables that meet defined specifications/thresholds; related to product design, testing, defects. Distinct from quality assurance. (Ch 6)

**Quiet quitting** — Employees doing the minimum to keep their job without extra effort — a named well-being/engagement warning sign. (Ch 11)

**RACI matrix** — A responsibility assignment matrix (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) ensuring exactly one person is Accountable per task. (Ch 14)

**Requirement** — A condition or capability necessary in a product/service/result to satisfy a business need. (Ch 7)

**Reserve** (see also contingency reserve, management reserve) — A provision in the project plan to mitigate cost/schedule risk. (Ch 9)

**Resource leveling** — A resource optimization technique adjusting dates to balance resource demand/supply; can change the critical path/end date. Contrast resource smoothing. (Ch 14)

**Resource smoothing** — A resource optimization technique adjusting only within existing float; never changes the critical path or end date. Contrast resource leveling. (Ch 14)

**Risk** — An uncertain event/condition that, if it occurs, has a positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) effect on objectives. (Ch 12)

**Risk appetite** — The degree of uncertainty an organization/individual will accept for a reward; quantified via risk threshold. (Ch 12)

**Risk Breakdown Structure (RBS)** — A hierarchical categorization of risk *sources* (e.g., Technical/Management/Commercial/External) — distinct from the WBS, which organizes deliverables. (Ch 14)

**Risk classification** — Known-Known (scope, not a risk) / Known-Unknown ("classic risk") / Unknown-Known ("hidden fact," knowledge exists elsewhere) / Unknown-Unknown ("emergent risk"). (Ch 12)

**Risk exposure** — An aggregate measure of the potential impact of all risks at a given point in time. (Ch 12)

**Risk threshold** — The measure of acceptable variation around an objective, reflecting risk appetite (e.g., ±5% vs. ±10% around a cost objective). (Ch 12)

**Schedule baseline** — The approved version of a schedule model, changeable only via formal change control, used as the basis for comparison. (Ch 7, Ch 8)

**Schedule Performance Index (SPI)** — EV / PV; greater than 1.0 means ahead of schedule. (Ch 14)

**Schedule Variance (SV)** — EV − PV; positive = ahead of schedule, negative = behind schedule. (Ch 14)

**Scope baseline** — In predictive environments, the approved formal scope documents (part of the Performance Measurement Baseline); in adaptive environments, defined per-iteration from prioritized requirements. (Ch 7)

**Scope (project)** — The work performed to deliver a product/service/result with specified features/functions; the most important component of a project's baseline since it encapsulates expected value. (Ch 7)

**SMART criteria** — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound — the bar for setting clear, effective goals/metrics. (Ch 6)

**Sourcing strategy** — The governance decision of whether to insource or outsource project work, considering schedule/scope/finance/resource/risk impacts. (Ch 6)

**Source selection methods** — Least cost, qualifications-only, quality-based, quality-and-cost-based, single-source, fixed-budget — chosen by procurement complexity/risk. (Ch 15)

**Stakeholder mapping models** — Power/Interest grid (simple), Stakeholder Cube (3D), Salience model (power/urgency/legitimacy or proximity — complex networks), Directions of Influence (upward/downward/outward/sideward). (Ch 14)

**Suitability filter** — A decision-making tool (not a rigid procedure) combining culture, team dynamics, and project factors to help choose predictive/adaptive/hybrid. (Ch 13)

**Tacit knowledge** — Personal, experience-based knowledge that is difficult to articulate, formalize, or transfer. (Ch 6)

**Tailoring** — The deliberate adaptation of project management approach, governance, and processes to fit a given environment and purpose of work, driven by principles plus organizational values/culture. (Ch 5, Ch 13)

**To-Complete Performance Index (TCPI)** — (BAC−EV)/(BAC−AC) or (BAC−EV)/(EAC−AC); the cost efficiency needed on remaining work to hit a budget goal. Greater than 1.0 = harder to complete. (Ch 14)

**Tuckman ladder** — Team development stages: Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning; can stall or regress. (Ch 11, Ch 14)

**Uncertainty** — Lack of understanding/awareness of issues, paths, or outcomes; deals with probabilities; doesn't automatically escalate into a tracked risk. (Ch 12)

**Value** — The excess of financial and nonfinancial benefits over investment gained from achieving portfolio/program/project goals; perceived differently by different stakeholders. (Ch 1, Ch 9)

**Value Breakdown Structure (VBS)** — A hierarchical structure connecting project scope and intended value to product scope, assigning value estimates to deliverables to drive prioritization. (Ch 7)

**Value delivery system** — The collection of strategic business activities (portfolios, programs, projects, products, operations) that build, sustain, or advance an organization. (Ch 1, Ch 2)

**Variance at Completion (VAC)** — BAC − EAC; the estimated cost difference at project completion. (Ch 14)

**Velocity** — The rate at which deliverables are produced, validated, and accepted within a predefined interval (adaptive approaches). (Ch 8)

**VRIO framework** — Value, Rarity, Imitability, Organization — evaluates whether a resource/capability is a genuine source of competitive advantage. (Ch 14)

**Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** — A hierarchical decomposition of total project scope into manageable work packages; forms part of the scope baseline in predictive/hybrid approaches. (Ch 7)

**WBS dictionary** — A supporting document elaborating each WBS component's scope description, milestones, responsible parties, resource needs, and acceptance criteria. (Ch 7)
